WebDev Simulation Reflections

Reflection as a Client

How did I decided what content/images to use?

In order decide, I kept what I wanted the end project to look like. I knew what I wish I was able to find when I was getting into powerwashing, and I know that it was difficult to find or compile that data. I also know that photos for powerwashing, especially public domain ones, are difficult to come by and are frequently more confusing. This caused my to lean more towards short blurbs with the ocassional image.

How did I communicate with my developer? Where there any issues with communicating?

To be completely honest, my mindset is "People work better without overbearing clients" and "If it isn't nessary, don't mention it.". As such, I communicated with my team only occasionally, such as when I was directly asked for clarity, or to update the team on how I wanted the wording for the HTML to be adjusted. I personally did not feel as if there were any issues, though I can easily how my team might be frustrated with my overall lack of communication.

Did my developer meet my expectations for my site?

My Lead Developer definently did. The home page looks amazing, and I know that he was responsible for that page. The other pages have been affected by the CSS but it appears as if there isn't even html for the page.

PowerWash Wisdom

Reflection as a Lead Developer

How did I manage my team and communicate tasks to my Junior Dev?

I was very on top of this for the first week. It was very challenging, as neither client or junior dev was responding to messages, and so there was no flow of communication. When I finally did hear back from the client, I immediatly passed the wireframe to dev, and completed a base version of the home page. The Junior Dev then began work on the child pages, referencing what I had done, and adding additional functionality to the header. I still do not know how he did that. It looks great and I need to take a look and figure it out. Beyond that, I did not communicate, outside of informing the client and my Junior Dev when the home page was completely finished, as we waited for the rest of the content.

How did I communicate with the client? Were there any issues?

We communicated almost exclusively through Microsoft Teams. Its where we provided updates and received the wireframe and page content. Outside of the long wait to be able to begin work, there were no issues.

How did I handle publishing my client's site and sharing it with the client?

I did not devote much thought to this. I simply made the repository, invited my junior dev, set up the folder structure, added each html file we would need, added the link to the about in Github, and copied the link over to the client via Teams.

Peanut Butter Home

Reflection as a Junior Developer

How did I communicate with my Team Lead? Were there any issues?

We primarily communicated through gmail in the early stages of the process. This meant that I fell behind quickly, as I was stuck waiting for my Lead to fork over the wireframe and content for a while, as he rarely checked his email. I reached as often as I ran into to roadblocks to figure out what approach I should take towards it. Outside of that, very minimal communication.

Did I know what was expected of me for the site?

Yes, I did, especially once we got the wireframe. The wireframe and the content was relatively straightforward, with the trouble stemming from the pure amount of it. I ended up cutting of portions of the content that was included in the document but not in the wireframe, and it still ended up being a lot of work. I was able to be a meaningful contributer to the project, as I set up the html files and the css files for our page, as well as importing the photos, and fixing the pathing for links and images everytime my Lead Dev reorganized the files.

Did I run into any issues with GutHub and adding changes to the site?

Github generally went pretty well. I made it a habit to push my changes anytime I finished a task, such as fixing pathing, importing photos, adding a file, finishing unifying headers and color schemes, or finished one of the segments for the child page, The main issues came in this last week and half. I believe my Lead Dev forgot to implement such a habit himself, as commits from him where few and far between, even though each one made significant progress. It did cause merge conflicts however, as he was operating with my changes applied, causing me to have to fix my CSS and the pathing for every page and image three times. This did not speed up the process, and why the child pages are still incomplete, dispite significant progress.

The Riders Handbook

General Reflections

What was the most challenging part of this project for me? Was I able to overcome it?

The most difficult part for me was easily dealing with other people. It is really frustrating to know that a project is dependant on you and your work, while also being completely unable to do anything due to anothers shortcomings. I ended up burning a lot of time on these projects simply waiting to hear back from my client, from my lead dev and from my junior dev.

What did I enjoy the most about this project?

I enjoyed the prep for the HTML the most. I loved how complex and different each section of The Riding Handbook's wireframe. It forced me to think of and plan for such complicated css while I was working on the HTML alone, such as establishing divs, ids, and classes for each section that I knew would need unique styling. I also enjoyed the brief moments of intense creative flow that experienced as I was working, effectively blocking out the rest of the world.

Do I feel more confident in workiing on real-world projects after this experience?

Yes and No, simultaneously. I feel more confident in my ability to a lot with a little, but significantly less confident in my ability to with people and my aggravation at being halted by them. I learned dearly how I can take a weeks worth of work, and grind it out in anywhere from 1 1/2 to 4 hours. I have done it and know that I could successfully do so again. I have also gained witness of how one person moving slowly or forgetting about their duties can slow down the process as a whole, effectively increasing the intensity of the time crunch and its associated anxiety.
